Project Overview & Context
Project Name: Trippin
1. The Problem (Why this exists)
Group trips, especially among university students and friends in Pakistan, suffer from a chaotic financial aftermath.

The Signal Issue: Popular spots (Khanpur Dam, hiking trails, northern areas) often have zero cellular coverage. Cloud-based apps like Splitwise fail here.

The "Who Paid?" Chaos: Cash is spent randomly (fuel, chai at a dhaba, boat rides). Keeping track of who paid for whom—and who just paid for themselves—becomes a messy debate at the end of the trip.

The Device Limit: Not everyone has a charged phone or wants to install a new app. Often, one person (the "Squad Leader") ends up paying and managing expenses for 3-4 others (like a couple or a specific car group).

2. The Solution (What we are building)
Trippin is an offline-first, peer-to-peer (P2P) expense splitting application. It mimics a digital ledger that lives on everyone's phone simultaneously, without needing a central internet server.

It works like a Mesh: One person starts a trip. Others join via a local connection (Bluetooth/Wi-Fi).

It creates a "Squad": A user can join a trip as a single individual OR as a "Squad Leader" representing multiple passive people (e.g., "Rafay + 3 friends").

It settles intelligently: At the end of the trip, the app uses a smart algorithm to simplify debts (e.g., instead of A paying B and B paying C, A just pays C).

3. The User Experience (The Vibe)
Target Audience: Gen-Z, University Students (GIKI, NUST, etc.), Travelers.

Visual Identity: Sleek, Dark Mode (Deep Blues/Purples). High contrast for sunlight visibility.

Key Feeling: Trust & Speed. The app must feel "solid"—if I log an expense, I need to know it’s saved and synced instantly, or queued safely if I disconnect.

4. Core Value Proposition
"Enjoy the trip, don't stress the bill. Trippin works where the internet doesn't."
